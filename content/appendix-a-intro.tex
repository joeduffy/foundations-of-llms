\PartIntroChapter{Introduction to Appendix A}

This appendix is deliberately provisional. It holds work whose long-term significance is not yet
settled, and it is revisited at every edition rather than left to accumulate. Papers whose impact
has since proven out graduate into the main chronological sequence: this edition, Mamba (2023) moved
to Part~VII, once hybrid linear-attention designs descended from it reached frontier production
models (Part~VIII). One new candidate was added in its place: Yang and colleagues' synthetic
bootstrapped pretraining, a credibly authored response to the data-scarcity constraint every
frontier lab now names, whose staying power is not yet clear. A paper earns a place here only when
it clears the same bar as everything else in this book; most candidates considered for this edition
did not, which is the expected outcome of holding the bar high rather than a shortfall to correct.

\section*{Papers in This Appendix}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Cunningham et al. (2023)} --- Sparse autoencoders for extracting interpretable features from language model activations.

\item \textbf{Krajewski et al. (2024)} --- Refined scaling laws for fine-grained mixture-of-experts architectures.

\item \textbf{Yuan et al. (2025)} --- Native Sparse Attention: hardware-aligned trainable sparse attention achieving substantial speedups while matching full attention quality.

\item \textbf{Qiu et al. (2025)} --- Gated attention: a per-head sigmoid gate on attention output that eliminates attention sinks and improves training stability.

\item \textbf{Yang et al. (2025)} --- Synthetic bootstrapped pretraining: synthesizing new training documents from learned inter-document correlations to offset the exhaustion of high-quality pretraining text.
\end{enumerate}
